How to read it

  • Strength is how good a team is now: opponent-adjusted EPA per play, success rate and scoring margin, in points against an average FBS team.
  • Résumé is what a team has earned: for each game, the result minus the chance a #25 team would have won that same game, at that site. A loss earns nothing however close. A win counts half for the win itself and half for how convincing it was, so a 1-point win is worth less than a clear one; margins past about three touchdowns add almost nothing.
  • FCS opponents are zero-credit games: they count as played and add nothing.
  • Strength reflects health now: skill players who missed a team's latest game are taken off, at the same half-value rate; hover a team's strength for its full-strength number.
  • Coaching: a coach's record of results above his roster's talent, across his career since 2018 and any school, adds to Strength at the size testing supports, fading as the season's own games show more of the coaching (about 0.4 points per point after 9 games). Career records against teams ranked top 25 at kickoff, and in one-score games, are scored against what was expected before kickoff. Shown, not scored: coaches differ against top teams more than luck explains, but a past record barely predicted the next such games. Fourth-down rank can be turned on under Columns.
  • Games are graded as faced: an opponent missing its quarterback or top playmakers counts as the weaker team it was that day, by half the missing players' value (the size measured from past games).
  • Head-to-head: when two teams have played and their ranking scores are close (within about five to eight spots near the top), the winner ranks directly above the loser, unless the result was reversed in a second meeting. If a team in between beat the winner, it moves up too and stays directly above (Penn State beat Washington, Washington beat Nebraska: Penn State, Washington, Nebraska).
  • The ranking is led by the résumé, who a team has beaten: résumé weight is games ÷ (games + 1), about 90% by midseason. Strength counts most early in the season, when there is little to go on, and is shown as its own rank for who would win a game tomorrow.
  • A red badge means results trail the stats by + spots; green means results are ahead of them.
